# AI Council 2.51.0

**Multi-provider AI council for Android** — independent answers, audits, deep analysis, local servers, source search, document import and report export.

The installed version is `2.51.0` (versionCode 118). Local brain notes through 2.51.0 add a fundamental-attribution check: explaining an action by character, nature, or "the type of person" without the situation, incentives, or the base rate of that behavior is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.50.0 add an overconfidence / under-calibration check: extreme certainty language ("definitely", "100%", "guaranteed", "no doubt") without hedging, base-rate language, or an explicit calibration statement is a hypothesis, not evidence that the claim is well-calibrated. Local brain notes through 2.49.0 add a Chesterton's-fence check: a recommendation to abolish a rule, process, check or team without naming why it exists is a hypothesis, not a reason to tear it down. Local brain notes through 2.48.0 add a belief-bias check: a conclusion accepted because it sounds plausible, is wanted, or is unwelcome, without a separate check that the argument is valid, is a hypothesis, not evidence. When two or more distortion hypotheses fire, the coordinator confidence is capped until a new observation resolves the top conflict. Local brain notes through 2.47.0 add an illusory-truth bias check: a conclusion from repetition, familiarity from hearing something many times, or "everyone has heard this" as evidence of truth without independent verification or new data is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.46.0 add a false-consensus bias check: a conclusion from projecting one's own view onto "everyone" or "most people" without independent data on the actual distribution or acknowledgment that one's own belief is not representative is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.45.0 add a social-proof / bandwagon bias check: a conclusion from popularity, "everyone is doing it", crowd consensus or "it is widely accepted" without independent evidence, mechanism, or acknowledgment that popularity is not evidence of truth or quality is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.44.0 add a recency-bias check: a conclusion from recent events, short streaks or "lately" without accounting for longer-term base rates or historical averages is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.43.0 add an exponential-growth bias check: a conclusion from a linear projection onto an exponential, compounding or multiplicative process without acknowledging the rate, doubling time, or that linear intuition fails is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.42.0 add a zero-risk bias check: a conclusion from a demand for absolute zero risk or complete safety without comparing residual risks, expected value, or acknowledging that absolute safety is usually impossible is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.41.0 add a Simpson-paradox check: an aggregate trend is not evidence that the same relationship holds inside every subgroup until the data are stratified and a reversal is ruled out. Local brain notes through 2.40.0 add a base-rate neglect check: a conclusion from a vivid case, similarity, or small sample without accounting for the population base rate or prior frequency is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.39.0 add an appeal-to-ignorance check: a conclusion from "no one has proven otherwise" or absence of evidence without positive support or an explicit burden-of-proof acknowledgment is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.38.0 add a selection-bias check: a conclusion from a convenient, self-selected or truncated sample without accounting for the selection process or population base rate is a hypothesis, not evidence. Local brain notes through 2.37.0 add an ecological-fallacy check: a group average, rate or correlation does not automatically apply to a specific individual without individual-level data. Local brain notes through 2.93.0 add a conjunction-fallacy check: a detailed story is not more probable than the broader class it belongs to, and a conjunction cannot exceed either of its parts. Local brain notes through 2.92.0 add an affect-heuristic check: liking or disgust is not a risk or a benefit. Notes through 2.91.0 add a scope-insensitivity check: the same strength of recommendation for 10 and 10,000, without a ratio, is not evidence that magnitude does not matter. Notes through 2.90.0 add an omission-bias check: "doing nothing is safer" is not a comparison of the harm of acting and the harm of not acting. Notes through 2.89.0 add an outcome-bias check: judging a decision solely by its outcome is not the same as judging the quality of the process and information available at decision time; a good process can produce a bad result, and a bad process can get lucky. Notes through 2.88.0 add a present-bias check: a nearer payoff or pain, without a horizon and a discount rate, is not a reason to act or refuse, and choosing the immediate gain on both sides without comparing the later outcome is not evidence. Notes through 2.87.0 add a probe-before-commit check: an irreversible step without a cheap reversible test that could flip the conclusion is not a reason to act, and a demand for more data without the observation that would change the decision is not an argument. Notes through 2.86.0 add a halo-effect check: a positive or negative trait (appearance, brand, status) is not evidence for an unrelated quality such as competence or reliability. Notes through 2.85.0 add a gambler's-fallacy check: a streak of independent outcomes does not make the opposite more probable, and the "law of averages" does not force a correction. Notes through 2.84.0 add a framing-effect check: the same decision framed as a gain versus a loss, or as relative percentages versus absolute numbers, can shift recommendations without the frame being acknowledged. Notes through 2.83.0 add an authority-bias check: status or "experts say" is not a substitute for substance, mechanisms, or base rates. Notes through 2.81.0 add a regression-to-the-mean check: an extreme observation is not a permanent trait, and subsequent measurements tend to be closer to the average. Notes through 2.79.0 add a principal–agent check and a false-dilemma check: a commission is not the decision-maker's payoff, and two named options are not the whole choice set. The local disagreement map now also covers expert 1 against the evidence auditor. Notes through 2.78.0 add loss aversion: a heavier feeling of loss is not a coefficient, and it is not a reason to skip expected value. On top of the four-slot council, the local synthesis protocol classifies a task as a decision, forecast, fact check, design, explanation, or general review and tells the experts how to answer. A levered or all-in question also gets an ergodicity check: a positive average is not a reason to bet a stake that one loss can wipe out. The coordinator must not raise confidence just because the wording agrees, and must not average incompatible claims. Decision questions end with DO / DON'T / CHECK FIRST. Logical-risk, calibration, pre-mortem, outside-view, sensitivity, tail-risk, opportunity-cost, Goodhart, sunk-cost, ergodicity, confirmation-bias, causal, principal–agent, false-dilemma, survivorship, regression-to-the-mean, authority-bias, framing-effect, gambler's-fallacy, halo-effect, probe-before-commit, present-bias, outcome-bias, omission-bias, scope-insensitivity, affect-heuristic, conjunction-fallacy, narrative-fallacy, second-order thinking, ecological-fallacy, selection-bias, appeal-to-ignorance, base-rate-neglect, Simpson-paradox, zero-risk-bias, exponential-growth-bias, recency-bias, social-proof-bias, false-consensus-bias, belief-bias, illusory-truth-bias, chesterton-fence, overconfidence and fundamental-attribution signals remain hypotheses that need context, not automatic verdicts.
